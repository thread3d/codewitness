**CodeWitness 1.0.26 removes three pieces of dead code the compiler cannot see and splits the report's densest
method. It exists because a second static analyser — GooseBuckle — was pointed at this repository, and the most
useful result of that exercise was discovering that most of what it reported was wrong: after checking every
finding against the source, 3 of its 144 were genuine defects, 12 were the tool's own false positives (filed in
its journal as `GB-86EB2C52`, `GB-281F773D`, `GB-4C945B5D` and `GB-17B09680`), and the remainder were design
choices or complexity measures on code that is deliberately long. What landed is what survived that check.**

## What changed

- **Three dead assignments are gone.** `PythonDependencyAnalyzer.analyseDependencies` built a `ProjectInfo` it
  never read — the method works from `context.projectRoot()` — and `VerificationEngine.recordDatabase` assigned
  each of its two `DATABASE_FIXTURE` evidence records to a local `evidence` that neither branch ever read. The
  `ledger.add(...)` calls stay, because the record is the point and the local never was. This is the shape
  `GB-QLT-UNU-001` exists to catch, and it caught one of the two in the engine and missed the other.

- **`HtmlReportWriter.appendEnvironment` is four methods instead of one.** It was 79 lines at cognitive
  complexity 59 and cyclomatic complexity 35 — the highest complexity density in the tree, ahead of
  `VerificationEngine.verify`'s 73 over 388 lines. It is now `appendEnvironment` (12 lines: resolve the snapshot
  and the run, open the section, make three calls) over `appendSnapshot` (the toolchain keys and the two
  timestamps, with the run's own clock as the fallback), `appendPhases` (the phase table, including the phases
  that were skipped) and `appendBudget` (the limits, the usage, and the warning when a limit truncated the run).
  The two `if/else` pairs became early returns, so every literal and the emission order are unchanged, and each
  new helper takes two or three parameters — under the threshold of the rule that would otherwise have flagged
  the extraction itself.

- **Measured effect.** GooseBuckle 0.2.5 over this tree reports 140 findings where it reported 144: the three
  dead locals and both `appendEnvironment` complexity findings are gone, and no finding was added. The
  duplication count is unchanged at 69, which is the intended outcome — nothing was rearranged to move a number.

## What this does not fix

- **The four long `verify` methods are untouched.** `VerificationEngine.verify` is 388 lines at cognitive 73 and
  cyclomatic 53; `GeneratedTestsStrategy.verify` is 201, `ExistingTestsStrategy.verify` 185 and
  `StaticAnalysisStrategy.verify` 154. All four are guard-clause sequences, and the obvious extraction trades a
  long-function finding for a seven-parameter helper — the same smell wearing a hat. Decomposing the engine's
  phase method is a change of its own, and it is not a drive-by in a release that is otherwise subtractive.

- **The two files over the project's own 2000-line threshold stay over it:** `VerificationEngine.java` at 2340
  and `SqliteVerificationStore.java` at 2186. The repository's notes record the engine as decomposed from 1,829
  lines, so this is a documented drift rather than a new one, and it deserves its own slice.

- **69 cross-adapter duplication findings remain.** They are real — `CppSourceScan` against `CSharpSourceScan`,
  `CppXml` against `CSharpXml`, the mutation testers and the test contexts — but they are a design decision
  across 16 sibling adapters, and the repair is to lift the shared shapes into `codewitness-adapter-support`,
  not 69 individual edits.

- **11 `GB-QLT-UNU-001` findings remain and are wrong.** Each names a local that is used later in the method that
  declares it. They disappear when GooseBuckle fixes `GB-86EB2C52`; they are not worked around here.

## Process note

**The refactor was aimed at the wrong function first, and a measurement was the reason.** GooseBuckle reported
`ConfigLoader.parse` as spanning 473 lines, which made it the single largest function in the tree and the obvious
place to start. Brace-matching that finding against the source showed the method closes at line 253 and is 129
lines — under the rule's own 150-line threshold — and that 11 of the 15 long-function findings on this tree name
an end line past the function, sometimes the end of the file. Only four were real, and the work moved to
`appendEnvironment`, which the same tool measures correctly and which has the worst complexity density of
anything it found.

The lesson is the one this project already applies to its own evidence, pointed at somebody else's: *"the project's
own tests pass"* is not proof, and neither is another tool's line count. A finding is evidence only after the
thing it describes has been looked at. Checking first cost one brace-matcher; refactoring first would have
rewritten a 129-line method that needed nothing, and the release note would have claimed it was 473 lines long.

## Compatibility

- No CLI command, flag, exit code, report key, SARIF profile, `codewitness.yaml` key or adapter-SPI change. Every
  change is inside a method body, a private helper or a declaration that nothing read.
- No public API change: `HtmlReportWriter`'s new methods are private, `ConfigLoader` is untouched, and both
  edited classes keep their signatures and their behaviour.
- The rendered `report.html` is unchanged, section for section and byte for byte — the split moved code, not text.
